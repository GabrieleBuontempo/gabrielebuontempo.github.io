# Hi! Welcome to my website  

<br>

I am Gabriele Buontempo, a PhD student in Economics at <a href="https://www.econ.lmu.de/en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">LMU Munich</a>.

My research interests are in **macroeconomic theory and empirics**. In particular, I study how heterogeneity in beliefs and expectations affects business cycles, inflation dynamics, and the transmission of monetary and fiscal policy.

_Abstract:_ Whose inflation expectations drive aggregate demand? Using the Survey of Consumer Expectations, I show that unconstrained households (Savers), whose expectations enter intertemporal consumption decisions, hold lower, more accurate, less volatile, and less dispersed inflation expectations than Hand-to-Mouth (HtM) households. Estimating a noisy-information model separately for the two groups, I find that during the 2021–2023 inflation surge all households underreact to inflation news and over-perceive the persistence of inflation. Compared with HtM households, Savers underreact more to news but over-perceive inflation persistence less. Introducing the estimated expectation parameters into a tractable heterogeneous-agent New Keynesian (THANK) model, I show that the expectations of Savers make the cumulative output contraction after a cost-push shock 45% larger than under full-information rational expectations. With the expectations of all households instead, the contraction is 98% larger than under full-information rational expectations. This result suggests that the choice of survey expectations used to discipline macroeconomic models matters for understanding the propagation of inflationary shocks.
